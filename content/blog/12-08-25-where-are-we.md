## Introduction

Well, it's been a while since I last made a post. A lot of things have happened and changed in tech, and in my life. I got a good job on a good team working with [Go](https://go.dev/), I got to go to Japan for a little over two weeks (something I've wanted to do since I was about eight years old), I've read a lot of books, started working on a side project I've had in mind for a while now, got heavily into [Balatro](https://www.playbalatro.com/) (which re-inspired me to start making games again — something I've always wanted to do and have only done [once before](https://williycole.github.io/07-challenge-rock-paper-scissors/) on a very small scale). I also got into sandbag lifting (so dang fun), started skateboarding more, and I'm not far off from getting my Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu purple belt. So yeah, it's been a cool year — but that's not all that happened.

During mid-summer my wife was laid off shortly after being told she wouldn't be. That really sucked, but she took it like a champion and has since started her own [web design business](https://popixelstudio.com/). Regardless, it was not a fair situation after she’d been told she was doing a great job and was part of the core team — one of those “it's not you, it's us” scenarios. Fast-forward a few months and I'm writing this roughly seven weeks after getting laid off myself. I was also blindsided. Not cool, but I'll save the details for another time.

## What's the point?

I'm mostly writing all of this to reflect, vent, and plant a flag in the digital ground that I can look back on a year from now. I've been getting paid to make things on computers since 2018. It started with maps and some automation, which led to a career pivot into “coding” (I thought frontend/websites), but I've mostly only ever been in backend-heavy software engineering roles for the last six years. Over that time I've worked at four places — all the way from red-tape hell with 500k employees down to a startup with about 100 folks, and a few places in between. I've been on seven different teams with seven different managers. I've led planning discussions, mentored other devs in my wheelhouse, worked alongside system architects to lay the groundwork for big ideas I spearheaded, been forward-deployed in the feature trenches, worked the firehose, worn the ops hat, and managed my own Docker/Kubernetes configs for microservices I ported out of unscalable implementations. I've done a lot, worked with a lot of people, and seen a crap-ton of management styles, scenarios, bad ideas, failures, successes, good ideas, and everything in between.

Yet here I am. No W-2, and to be honest, a little burned out in the age of AI — arguably one of the weirdest times in tech in the last twenty years. Very much a “damned if you use it, damned if you don't” moment, at least from my perspective. The old heads (not all of them) tend to be skeptical and really don't like anyone under them using it. There’s no agreed-upon “here's how we use it” policy across the industry, yet all the CEOs love it and can’t help but see dollar signs in savings and sales. It's quite the dilemma and pretty dang toxic if you ask me. Tech is weird at the moment, and I don't see it changing anytime soon.

Having seen and done so much, I can't help but think this isn't worth it. This constant uphill grind only to be tossed aside unfairly, all while being told “dance, monkey, dance,” with very little support. Is it worth it? Outsourcing, underpaying, belittling, blaming, poor communication, constant churn, ego, tech-bro attitudes — none of this sounds good, right? If I described the software running your next flight with any of those adjectives, you probably wouldn't want to get on that plane. So why is it that, of all the places I've worked and people I've worked with, I can count on maybe two hands the folks who genuinely cared about doing a good job, helping others, and seeing teams, teammates, and the company grow and win? Not just good people — amazing people. Y'all know who you are.

What is the point? A paycheck? Frustration? Comfort? Products? A W-2? I'm not sure. We all have to pay our bills and most of us want to do a good job at the end of the day. But given all the crazy, I can't help but think there's a better way. I'm not a perfect person or a perfect engineer, but I do care about people, I do see what I do as a craft, and I personally want what I build to be right and fast. That said, I'm unwilling to be a jerk or throw others under the bus just to save my own skin. I never wanted to be a 10× engineer, and I don't really care to define myself solely as a programmer. I just like having a craft, tinkering, polishing, and doing a good job.

## Where are we at and where are we going?

So where does that leave me? Where does that leave other engineers like me? To be honest, I'm not sure. At the moment, I'm working for myself — coding, consulting, and a few other things. Big Tech is big annoying right now, and I just want to build things unhindered for a while.

So I guess to some degree I'm taking a career break (can we still call it that???). I'm going solo for a bit, and honestly, I wouldn't be surprised if a lot more devs end up doing the same in the coming years. As a dev with six years of real experience I have a ton of skills now. Sure, there's still plenty I don't know and haven't built. Yeah, I couldn't just hop into Google and hit the ground running (news flash — most folks can't), but that doesn't matter for most businesses. Most companies don't need to operate like Google; most just need a little software and a little automation, not full-time devs forever. Honestly, in the years to come I think the need for a competent dev to come in, help out for a while, and then check in periodically will only grow. That's where I plan to spend my skills for the time being.

AI is only going to even the skill gap further for everyone. I think devs should take time to think about that and what they actually want to do with their lives and skills. To be honest, coding is a means to an end. Sure — tinker, build, make that game, work your gig, etc. — but take it as a lesson from me rambling here: your W-2 isn't safe. You are a valued employee until you're not. Don't count on getting comfortable in your job or your skills. Get comfortable being uncomfortable and have fun with it.

That's it. Rant done.

I'll be doing more writing here about solo web dev and solo game dev in the future.
