## Introduction

Well it's been a while since I last made a post, a lot of things have happened
and changed in tech, and in my life. I got a good job on a good team working
with [go](https://go.dev/), I got to go to Japan for a little over two weeks, something
I've wanted to do  since I was like 8 years old. I've read a lot of books, started
working on a side project I've had an idea about for a while now, got heavily into
[Blatro](https://www.playbalatro.com/) which re-inspired me to start making games
again, something I've always wanted to do, and have only done [once before](https://williycole.github.io/07-challenge-rock-paper-scissors/)
on a very small scale. I also got into sandbag lifting(so dang fun), started
skateboarding more, and I'm not far off from getting my Brazilian Jujitsu Purple
belt. So yea, its been a cool year, but that's not all that happened.

During mid summer my wife was laid off shortly after being told she wouldn't be.
That really sucked, but she took it like a champion and has since then started
her own [web design business](https://popixelstudio.com/), irregardless it was
not a fair situation after having been told she was doing a great job and apart
of the core team. One of those its not you its us scenarios. Fast forward a few
months and I'm writing this roughly 7 weeks after getting laid off myself. I was
also blindsided, an not cool but I'll save the details of that for a future date.

## Whats the point?

I'm mostly writing all of this to reflect, vent, and plant a flag in the digital
ground that I can look back at a year from now. I've been getting paid to make things
on computers since 2018. It was maps and some automation, which led to a career pivot
into "coding"(I thought frontend/websites), but I've mostly only ever been in  backend
heavy SE roles for the last 6 years that I've been getting paid to do this kind
of work. Over that time I've worked 4 places all the way from all the red tape
with 500k employees, down a start up with 100 or so folks, and a few places in
between. I've on 7 different teams, **with 7 different managers**, I've led planning
discussions, mentored other devs in areas that where my wheel house, worked
alongside System Architects to lay the ground work for big ideas that I spear headed,
been forward deployed in the feature space, worked the fire house, worn the ops
hat, and managed my own docker/k8 configs for microservices I ported from their
places cemented between unscalable implementations. I've done a lot and I've worked
with a lot of people, and I've seen a crap ton of things, management styles,
scenarios, bad ideas, failures, successes, good ideas and again, everything in between.

Yet here I am. No W2, and a to be honest, a little burned in the age of ai,
arguably one of the weirdest times in tech over the last 20 years. Very much so
a damned if you use it damned if you don't use it time, at least from my perspective.
The old heads(not all of them) tend to be skeptical and really not like anyone under
them to use it, there's not agreed upon "here's how we use it" across the board,
and all the CEOs love it and can't help but see dollar signs in savings and sales.
It's quite the dilemma and pretty dang toxic if you ask me. Tech is so weird at
the moment and I don't see it changing anytime soon.

Having seen and done so much I can't help but think this isn't worth it.
This constant up hill grind to only be tossed to the side unfairly, all while
being told "dance monkey dance", with very little to no support. Is it worth it?
Outsourcing, underpaying, belittling, blaming, poorly communicating, constant
changing, ego, tech bro attitudes, none of this sounds good right?
I mean, if I described the software used by your pilot on your next flight
with any of these adjectives you probably wouldn't want to get on that plane right?
If that's the case, why is it of all the places I've worked in tech and people
I've worked with why is it that I can only count about 20 folks that really
cared to do a good job, help others, and see teams, teammates, and the company
grow and win. Not just good people, but amazing people, ya'll know who you are.

What is the point? A pay check, frustration, comfort, products, a W2? I'm not sure
I mean we all have to pay our bills but and want to do a good job at the end of
the day and hopefully most of us do. But given all the crazy, I can't help but
think there's a better way.I mean I'm not a perfect person or perfect engineer
for that matter, but I do care about people, I do see what I do as a craft, and I
personally want what I do to be right, and fast. That' said, I'm unwilling to
be a jerk and throw others under the bus just for the sake of my own skin. I
never wanted to be a 10x engineer and I don't really care to define myself as a
programmer. I just like having a craft, tinkering, polishing, and doing a good job.

## Where are we at and where are we going?

So where does that leave me? Where does that leave other engineers like me? To be
honest, I'm not sure. At the moment, I'm working for myself, coding, consulting,
and a few other things. Big tech is big annoying at the moment and I just want
to build things unhindered for a while.

So I guess to some degree I'm taking a career(can we still call it that???)break.
I'm doing things solo for a bit, and honestly, I wouldn't be surprised if more devs
end up doing that in the future. As dev with 6 years of experience I have a ton
of skills now. Sure there's still plenty I don't know and haven't built. Yea I couldn't
just hop in a google and hit the ground running(news flash most folks can't) but
that doesn't. Most business don't need to operate like google, most businesses
just need a little software and a little automation, not full time devs.
Honestly in the years to come I think the need for a dev to come into a business
and just help out for a while or periodically will only grow. That's where I
plan to spend my skills for the time being.

AI is only going to even the skill gap for all devs. I think devs should take
time to think about that and what they want to do with their life/skills.
To be honest coding is a means to an end. Sure, tinker, build, make that game,
work you gig, etc, but take it as a lesson from me rambling here. You're W2
isn't safe. You are a valued employee, until your not. Don't count on getting
comfortable in your job or you skills. Get comfortable being uncomfortable and
have fun with it. That's it, rant done. I'll be doing more writing here talking
about solo web dev/solo game dev in the future.
